## 1. Abstract paragraph on the evaluation (drop-in replacement)

> The evaluation was performed as a synthetic software experiment in SIM mode.
> Five scenario configurations, the determinism of repeated runs, the separation
> of random streams across replicates, artifact integrity and data-quality gates
> were examined. A suite of 40 automated checks passed in full, and 86 behavioural
> reference values of the executable specification were reproduced exactly. All
> numerical outputs characterise the behaviour of the software model under stated
> assumptions; they are not measurements of a physical cyber range.

## 2. Methods — study design

> This study is a computational, pre-experimental investigation and a
> reproducibility check of a modular software prototype of a cyber-range digital
> twin. The experimental part was executed exclusively in SIM mode; every numerical
> output was produced by the software model. Throughout, verification of the
> implementation is separated from validation against a physical system.
>
> The campaign was a controlled software campaign. The **unit of analysis was an
> individual software run with a fixed configuration, seed and replicate
> identifier**, never an individual telemetry row. The executed campaign comprised
> five scenarios — `baseline-quiet`, `wan-failover`, `cyber-campaign`,
> `power-outage` and `compound-challenge` — a three-replicate demonstration run, an
> eight-point exploratory Latin-hypercube design of experiments over five factors,
> and a demonstration Monte Carlo campaign with sequential stopping. In total the
> campaign contains 16 independent run/replicate units.
>
> Scenario interventions changed the states and features of the software model and
> generated **no external attack traffic**. External egress and writes to physical
> hardware were forbidden by the execution policy and the prohibition is exercised
> by automated safety tests. Hardware-in-the-loop mode was unavailable in software
> while critical parameters remained unknown.

## 3. Methods — computational environment and reproducibility

> The modular package was executed under CPython 3.11.15 on Linux x86_64 with no
> third-party dependencies. The campaign used base seed 20260903, with named random
> streams derived from the triple (seed, replicate identifier, namespace).
> Configuration, source code, environment and resulting artifacts were identified by
> hashes, and run directories were never overwritten.
>
> Each run produced separate telemetry, ground-truth, alert, summary and manifest
> files. Ground truth was generated by the scenario controller and was not used as
> an input to the feature pipeline, which limits leakage but does not make the
> scenario labels an independent physical truth. Each manifest records the
> configuration hash, the engine source hash, the runtime fingerprint and the
> SHA-256 of every artifact, so that every reported number is traceable along the
> chain parameter → run → artifact → metric → claim.

## 4. Methods — remote organisation of the work

> Because of the security and logistical constraints of martial law and the
> full-scale armed aggression of the Russian Federation against Ukraine, the
> preparation of configurations, the entry of model parameters, the execution of
> SIM runs and the analysis of artifacts were carried out remotely. Remote entry of
> configuration parameters is a way of organising software work; it is not a source
> of physical measurements. Any value not confirmed by physical inventory, by a
> device datasheet or by real telemetry retained the status SYNTHETIC_DEMO or
> UNKNOWN.

## 5. Methods — parameter and data provenance

> Each parameter carried both a deployment status and an evidence status. In the
> demonstration inventory 194 parameters were SYNTHETIC_DEMO and four were UNKNOWN;
> no parameter had the status MEASURED, VENDOR_SPEC, DERIVED or ASSUMED. The
> unknown parameters were the battery chemistry and the number of parallel branches
> of the pack, and the VPN MTU and protocol; they block hardware-in-the-loop
> execution in software until they are measured.
>
> Every resulting record of this campaign is of class *synthetic*. No record was
> merged with field or measured data. Telemetry gaps in these data are
> **deliberately injected synthetic gaps**, not losses of real measurements; gaps
> are preserved as gaps and never silently imputed, and non-finite values are
> rejected by the data-contract layer.
>
> Every dataset carries the provenance vector `stimulus_origin =
> scripted_synthetic`, `observation_origin = simulator_output`, `label_origin =
> scenario_controller`, `curation_origin = automated_pipeline`, `analysis_origin =
> derived_metric`. The values `measured`, `physical_sensor` and
> `cyber_range_instrumentation` do not occur in the deposit.

## 6. Methods — quality control and verification

> Software verification covered unit, property, contract, determinism, safety,
> integration, calibration and performance checks; **40 of 40 passed**. Strict data
> schemas, the prohibition of non-finite values, blanked measurement fields in gap
> rows, event causality, the separation of random-number streams and artifact
> integrity were checked separately.
>
> Passing the tests is interpreted as evidence of **verification**: the
> implementation conforms to the stated specification and its invariants. It is not
> evidence of physical validation, and it does not establish the plausibility of any
> particular value of round-trip time, autonomy, Wi-Fi behaviour or field detector
> effectiveness.

## 7. Methods — statistical interpretation

> The statistical results are exploratory and demonstrative. Telemetry rows within
> a single run are dependent and are not treated as independent observations. One
> run per scenario does not support an estimate of between-run variability;
> intervals computed within the campaign are descriptive. The eight-point design
> over five factors was used for a preliminary search for sensitive parameters, not
> for a final ranking of causal effects. Monte Carlo with early stopping at a small
> number of replicates is presented as a demonstration of the pipeline, not as
> confirmatory evidence.

## 8. Results

> The modular implementation executed the five planned synthetic scenarios and a
> three-replicate demonstration run. All 40 automated checks passed and the 86
> enumerated behavioural reference values of the specification were reproduced
> exactly. The engine source hash of the executed tree,
> `2136f8f4be6e300c272a52056e15038260f33d67e0283cadde99939a09b24549`, is identical
> to the hash recorded for the previous reproduction.

A negative ΔSoC denotes charging, a positive value discharge. Scenario telemetry
totals 7,627 rows.

> `baseline-quiet` produced no alerts: the transparent rule baseline has no false
> positives on the nominal synthetic workload. `cyber-campaign` showed a precision
> of 1.0 at a recall of 0.1965 — the rules capture only intensive reconnaissance
> while latent stages remain undetected; this is a property of the model's internal
> scenario-ground-truth loop and is not a statement about detector quality in the
> field. `power-outage` produced 797 steps with load groups shed while group I was
> preserved, and 53 steps with latched protection trips. `compound-challenge`
> simultaneously disrupted power, transport and telemetry: modelled availability at
> site A fell to 91.32 %, and the data-quality gates still passed because telemetry
> defects were flagged rather than concealed. The worst modelled autonomy across
> the scenarios was 54.31 min and the maximum modelled pack temperature 24.1 °C;
> both are outputs of a gray-box surrogate, not measured characteristics of a
> battery system.

> The demonstration run produced 5,422 rows (1,806 / 1,811 / 1,805 per replicate)
> with all gates passing; modelled availability was 96.124 % at site A and 100 % at
> site B, with RTT p95 of 71.915 ms and 76.714 ms respectively and 30 failover steps
> at site A. Differing row counts across replicates follow from independent random
> streams and injected synthetic telemetry gaps, not from data loss.
>
> The eight-point Latin-hypercube design varied initial state of charge, critical
> load, failover delay, nominal offered load and detector threshold (design hash
> `21ba9401…c594`). The observed range of RTT p95 at site A was 71.688–73.06 ms.
> The design is an exploratory demonstration of the pipeline; no factor is
> described as dominant.
>
> The Monte Carlo campaign on the metric `network.site_a.rtt_p95_ms` stopped at the
> fifth replicate on the target half-width criterion, with an estimate of 71.8948 ms
> and a cluster-bootstrap interval of [71.7355; 72.0784] (normal approximation
> [71.7183; 72.0713]). Because the unit of analysis is the run, the interval is
> constructed by cluster bootstrap. This characterises the internal stability of
> the model, not field uncertainty.

## 9. Results — evidence boundary

> The archived campaign passed 40/40 automated software checks and reproduced 86/86
> enumerated behavioural reference values. These results demonstrate internal
> software verification and reproducibility relative to the executable
> specification. They do **not** constitute calibration or validation against the
> physical UMSF cyber range. The 13,049 rows of the scenario and demonstration runs
> are dependent time-series records across eight independent run/replicate units,
> not 13,049 independent observations.

**Disclosed discrepancy.** The specification prints a truncated reference engine
source hash `925c24c6…`, whereas the source tree extracted from that same
specification hashes to `2136f8f4…4549`. The difference is a byte-level
consequence of extracting code out of Markdown (trailing newlines) and is not a
behavioural difference; the trees are not claimed to be byte-identical.

## 10. Contribution statement (defensible at the current evidence level)

> The novelty of this work lies not in the separate use of a digital twin, edge
> analytics or known statistical models, but in their **evidence-traceable
> integration** into a single methodology for pre-experimental verification of
> cyber resilience. Its distinguishing feature is that the boundary between
> synthetic, documented, measured and unknown is implemented not as a textual
> caveat but as an executable policy: unknown critical parameters block
> hardware-in-the-loop execution, external egress and hardware writes are
> forbidden, and gaps are preserved as gaps rather than replaced by invented
> values.
>
> The specific contributions are: (i) a single causal model of the network, Wi-Fi,
> energy, telemetry and cyber-security subsystems; (ii) machine-checkable
> provenance of parameters and results; (iii) deterministic named random streams
> with replicate separation; (iv) a separate scenario-generated ground truth with
> leakage control into the feature pipeline; (v) safe feature-level threat
> modelling without exploit code or external targets; (vi) automated contract,
> safety, data-quality and readiness gates; and (vii) a reproducible chain from
> parameter to run to artifact to metric to claim.

## 11. Limitations

> The principal limitation is the absence of physical calibration and of
> independent sim-to-real validation. Emulation, replay of real telemetry,
> hardware-in-the-loop execution, physical tests of the network infrastructure and
> shadow-mode evaluation were outside the scope of this release. The battery model
> is a gray-box surrogate, and the radio, VPN, WAN and thermal behaviours are
> simplified. Scenario metrics cannot be generalised to production networks,
> physical sensors, real battery systems or an adaptive adversary. No parameter in
> the reported inventory is classified as MEASURED; 194 are SYNTHETIC_DEMO and four
> are UNKNOWN. Consequently the archived metrics must not be interpreted as field
> performance of the physical network, Wi-Fi, VPN, backup-power system or
> detectors. Cross-platform reproducibility beyond CPython 3.11 on Linux x86_64
> has not been tested.

## 12. Conclusion paragraph

> The developed prototype provides reproducible pre-experimental verification of
> scenarios and scientific artifacts for a cyber range. Its strengths are explicit
> parameter provenance, deterministic runs, an executable claim boundary and
> machine-checkable integrity of every artifact. The necessary next stage is
> physical inventory, collection of calibration data, an independent validation
> sample, implementation of the emulation, replay and hardware-in-the-loop modes,
> and a pre-defined bridge experiment. Only after that stage may conclusions be
> drawn about actual switchover times, coverage, autonomy or field detector
> accuracy.
